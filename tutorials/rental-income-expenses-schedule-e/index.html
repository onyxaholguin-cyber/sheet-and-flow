How to Track Rental Income and Expenses by Schedule E Line (Free Spreadsheet Method)

Updated 2026-09-24 · Sheet & Flow

If you own a rental or two, the IRS wants your year summarized on Schedule E: rents received on line 3, then expenses on lines 5–19 (advertising, insurance, repairs, taxes and so on).

The easiest way to get there is to decide the line when you record the transaction, not in a panic in March. This tutorial shows the three-part setup we use.

1. A category table that knows the Schedule E line

Make a tab called Categories with two columns: the category name you'll pick from a dropdown, and its line code.

Category Line
Rent Line 3
Advertising Line 5
Auto and travel Line 6
Cleaning and maintenance Line 7
Insurance Line 9
Legal and professional fees Line 10
Management fees Line 11
Mortgage interest Line 12
Repairs Line 14
Supplies Line 15
Taxes Line 16
Utilities Line 17
Capital improvement None

Use text codes like Line 9, not bare numbers. A bare 9 can be stored as a number in one cell and as text in another, and then SUMIFS silently misses it. Line 9 is always text.

Capital improvements (a new roof, a new furnace) are not deducted on the expense lines; they are depreciated. Mark them None so they stay out of the totals and hand them to your preparer.

2. A transactions table

Put one row per payment in or out on a Transactions tab:

A B C D E F
1 Date Property Category Amount Line Year
2 2026-01-03 Maple St Rent 1450 formula formula

The two formula columns:

E2:  =IFERROR(VLOOKUP(C2, Categories!$A$2:$B$50, 2, FALSE), "?")
F2:  =YEAR(A2)

Fill them down. Any category typo shows as ?, so you can spot it instantly. Add a dropdown to column C (Data → Data validation → List from a range → Categories!A2:A50) and typos mostly disappear.

3. The Schedule E summary

On a Summary tab, put property names across row 1 (B1, C1, …) and line codes down column A (A3, A4, …). One formula fills the whole grid:

=SUMIFS(Transactions!$D$2:$D$2000, Transactions!$B$2:$B$2000, B$1,
        Transactions!$E$2:$E$2000, $A3, Transactions!$F$2:$F$2000, 2026)

The $ signs are the whole trick. B$1 keeps the row fixed so the property follows the column. $A3 keeps the column fixed so the line follows the row. Copy it across and down.

Total expenses (line 20) is the sum of the expense rows. Income or loss (line 21) is line 3 minus line 20.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing the tax year. Always filter on the Year column, and start a fresh copy each January.
  • Recording loan payments as an expense. Only the interest part goes on line 12. The principal is not deductible.
  • Security deposits. A deposit you expect to return is not income when you receive it. Track deposits separately.
