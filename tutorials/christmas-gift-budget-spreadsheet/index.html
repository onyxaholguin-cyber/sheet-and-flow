Christmas Gift Budget Spreadsheet: Per-Person Budgets, Gift Status and Black Friday Dates (Excel & Google Sheets)

Updated 2026-10-16 · Sheet & Flow

Short on time? The Christmas & Holiday Budget Planner ($9) is the finished, tested version of what this tutorial builds.

Most holiday overspending isn't one big purchase. It's a dozen "small" gifts nobody added up until January. The fix is a sheet that knows the difference between a gift you're thinking about and a gift you've paid for, and that totals both per person.

This tutorial builds that sheet in three tabs: Settings, People and Gifts. It works the same in Excel, Google Sheets and LibreOffice, using only SUMIFS, IF, AND/OR, N, DATE, WEEKDAY and MOD. To test it, we built a workbook with these exact formulas, recalculated it in LibreOffice, and compared every cell to the worked example below and to an independent Python model. We also checked the holiday dates for every year from 2024 to 2040.

1. The Gifts tab

Row 1 holds the headers: Person, Gift, Status, Estimate, Actual, Cost, Spent. You type columns A–E. Status is one of Idea, Bought, Wrapped, Given (add a dropdown with Data → Data validation). Columns F and G are formulas. Fill them down as far as you need.

F2 (Cost):  =IF(AND(D2="",E2=""),"",IF(E2<>"",E2,D2))
G2 (Spent): =IF(OR(C2="Bought",C2="Wrapped",C2="Given"),N(F2),0)

Cost uses the actual price once you've typed one and the estimate until then, so a gift you've bought but whose receipt you haven't logged still counts. Spent counts the cost only once the gift is past the Idea stage. N() turns a blank cost into 0, so an empty row adds nothing.

2. The People tab

Row 1 holds the headers: Person, Budget, Planned, Spent, Left, Status. Type names and budgets in A and B, then fill C–F down:

C2 (Planned): =SUMIFS(Gifts!F:F,Gifts!A:A,A2)
D2 (Spent):   =SUMIFS(Gifts!G:G,Gifts!A:A,A2)
E2 (Left):    =B2-D2
F2 (Status):  =IF(B2="","",IF(D2>B2,"Over budget",IF(C2>B2,"Plan is over budget","OK")))

Planned includes ideas, so it warns you before you buy. "Plan is over budget" means your list for that person already costs more than their budget, even though you haven't spent it yet. Person names must match exactly on both tabs (a dropdown on Gifts!A pointing at People!A avoids typos).

3. The Settings tab

B1: 2026                       (year)
B2: 600                        (total gift budget)
B4 (Thanksgiving, US): =DATE(B1,11,1)+MOD(5-WEEKDAY(DATE(B1,11,1)),7)+21
B5 (Black Friday):     =B4+1
B6 (Cyber Monday):     =B4+4
B7 (Days to Christmas): =MAX(0,DATE(B1,12,25)-TODAY())
B9 (Total spent):      =SUM(Gifts!G:G)
B10 (Remaining):       =B2-B9
B11 (Given to people): =SUM(People!B:B)
B12 (Not yet assigned): =B2-B11

Format B4:B6 as dates. The Thanksgiving formula finds the first Thursday of November. WEEKDAY returns 1 for Sunday through 7 for Saturday, and Thursday is 5, so MOD(5-WEEKDAY(...),7) is the number of days from November 1 to that Thursday. Adding 21 jumps three weeks to the fourth Thursday. Change B1 and every date moves.

Worked example

Settings: year 2026, budget $600. People: Mom $120, Dad $80, Emma $150, Liam $140.

Mom Novel Idea 25.00 25.00 0.00
Dad Cordless drill Bought 139.00 89.00 89.00 89.00
Emma Ice skates Bought 80.00 74.50 74.50 74.50

Liam LEGO set Given 79.99 59.99 59.99 59.99

Liam Fun socks Idea 15.00 15.00 0.00

Mom 120.00 79.00 54.00 66.00 OK

Settings results: Thanksgiving Thu Nov 26, 2026, Black Friday Nov 27, Cyber Monday Nov 30. Total spent $347.49, remaining $252.51, given to people $490.00, not yet assigned $110.00.

Dad's drill was a Black Friday deal, but $89 is still more than his $80 budget. Liam hasn't overspent yet, but his idea list has. Those are exactly the two things the sheet is meant to catch.

Common mistakes

  • Counting ideas as spent. If your total looks too high, check that Spent uses the status test and not just the cost.
  • Name typos. "Mom" and "Mom " (with a trailing space) are different people to SUMIFS. Use a dropdown.
  • Forgetting the estimate. Leave Actual blank until you know it; Cost falls back to the estimate, so bought gifts are never counted as $0.
  • Hard-coding Black Friday. It isn't a fixed date. Use the formula so next year's copy just works.
