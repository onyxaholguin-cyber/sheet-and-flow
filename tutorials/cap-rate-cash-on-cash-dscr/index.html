Cap Rate vs. Cash-on-Cash vs. DSCR: A Worked Rental Example

Updated 2026-09-24 · Sheet & Flow

Three ratios come up in every rental conversation, and they answer three different questions:

Metric Formula Question it answers
Cap rate NOI ÷ price How good is the property, ignoring financing?
Cash-on-cash Annual cash flow ÷ cash invested What does my cash earn this year?
DSCR NOI ÷ annual debt service Can the rent carry the loan? (Lenders care about this one)

All three start from NOI, net operating income: what the property earns after operating expenses but before the mortgage.

The example property

A single-family rental listed at $250,000:

  • Rent $2,100/month. Vacancy allowance 5%.
  • Taxes $3,000/yr and insurance $1,400/yr.
  • Repairs 5% and CapEx reserve 5% of rent. Property management 8% of collected rent.
  • 25% down, 7% interest, 30 years. Closing costs $6,000, and $4,000 of repairs up front.

Step 1: NOI

  • Gross scheduled rent: $2,100 × 12 = $25,200
  • Vacancy (5%): −$1,260, so effective gross income = $23,940
  • Operating expenses:
  • taxes + insurance = $4,400
  • repairs + CapEx (10% of $25,200) = $2,520
  • management (8% of $23,940) = $1,915.20
  • total $8,835.20
  • NOI = $23,940 − $8,835.20 = $15,104.80

Step 2: Cap rate

$15,104.80 ÷ $250,000 = 6.04%. That's respectable for many markets. If you stopped here, you'd think it's a fine deal.

Step 3: The loan

  • Loan = 75% of $250,000 = $187,500.
  • At 7% for 30 years the payment is $1,247.44/month, or $14,969.31/year of debt service.

Step 4: Cash flow and cash-on-cash

  • Annual cash flow = $15,104.80 − $14,969.31 = $135.49, about $11/month.
  • Cash invested = $62,500 down + $6,000 closing + $4,000 repairs = $72,500.
  • Cash-on-cash = $135.49 ÷ $72,500 = 0.19%.

Step 5: DSCR

$15,104.80 ÷ $14,969.31 = 1.01. Many lenders want 1.20–1.25 for investment loans. At 1.01, one bad month wipes out the year's cash flow.

What the three numbers are telling you

The property is fine (6% cap rate), but at 7% interest with 25% down, the financing eats almost all of the income. To make it work you'd need a lower price, higher rent, a bigger down payment or a cheaper loan. That's exactly what sensitivity tables are for.

Also check break-even occupancy: (operating expenses + debt service) ÷ gross rent = (8,835.20 + 14,969.31) ÷ 25,200 = 94.5%. If the house sits empty for more than about 20 days a year, you're paying to own it.
