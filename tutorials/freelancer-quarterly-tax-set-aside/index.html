Freelancer Taxes: A Simple Set-Aside Method and the 2026 Quarterly Due Dates

Updated 2026-09-24 · Sheet & Flow

When you freelance, nobody withholds tax from your pay. The fix most freelancers settle on is boring and effective: every time you get paid, move a fixed percentage of profit to a separate savings account, then pay estimated tax from that account each quarter.

Step 1: Pick a set-aside percentage

Your real tax depends on income tax brackets, self-employment tax, deductions, state tax and your household. So there is no universal number.

Many freelancers start with 25–30% of profit and adjust after their first tax return. A tax professional can give you a number for your situation in one short meeting, and it's worth doing once.

"Profit" means money received minus business expenses. It is not revenue.

Step 2: Know the estimated-tax periods

The IRS periods are not calendar quarters:

Period Covers Nominal due date 2026/27 due date
1 Jan 1 – Mar 31 April 15 Wed, Apr 15, 2026
2 Apr 1 – May 31 June 15 Mon, Jun 15, 2026
3 Jun 1 – Aug 31 September 15 Tue, Sep 15, 2026
4 Sep 1 – Dec 31 January 15 (next year) Fri, Jan 15, 2027

Period 2 is only two months and period 4 is four months. When a due date lands on a weekend or holiday it moves to the next business day, so always confirm on irs.gov (Form 1040-ES).

Step 3: A worked example

Say that in period 3 (Jun–Aug) you were paid $14,000 and had $2,200 of business expenses.

  • Profit = $14,000 − $2,200 = $11,800
  • Set-aside at 25% = $2,950

If you moved 25% of profit each time a payment arrived, the $2,950 is already sitting in the tax account on September 15.

Step 4: Track it in a spreadsheet

For each period, sum payments and expenses between the start and end dates:

E5 (profit):     =SUMIFS(Payments!$C:$C, Payments!$A:$A, ">="&B5, Payments!$A:$A, "<="&C5)
                  - SUMIFS(Expenses!$G:$G, Expenses!$A:$A, ">="&B5, Expenses!$A:$A, "<="&C5)
F5 (set-aside):  =MAX(0, E5) * $B$2

Here Payments has the date in column A and the amount in C. Expenses has the date in A and the deductible amount in G. $B$2 holds your set-aside percentage.

B5 and C5 are the period's start and end dates. MAX(0, …) stops a loss-making period from producing a negative set-aside.

Add columns for estimated payment made and date paid so you can see at a glance whether each period is covered.

Common mistakes

  • Using revenue instead of profit. You over-save, which is safe but ties up cash.
  • Forgetting period 2 is short. June 15 arrives only two months after April 15.
  • Spending the tax account. Keep it at a different bank if you're tempted.
