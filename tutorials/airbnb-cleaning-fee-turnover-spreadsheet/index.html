Airbnb Cleaning Fee Spreadsheet: Turnover Margin, Net Cleaning Income and Break-Even Fee (Excel & Google Sheets)

Updated 2026-10-16 · Sheet & Flow

Short on time? The Short-Term Rental Analyzer ($19) is the finished, tested version of what this tutorial builds.

Most hosts set a cleaning fee that "looks fair" next to what guests pay elsewhere, then treat the difference between fee and cleaner cost as free money. On platforms where the host fee applies to the cleaning fee too, that difference can be negative even when the sticker fee is higher than what you pay the cleaner. The useful number is net margin per stay after the host fee, not fee minus cost.

This tutorial builds a one-tab cleaning / turnover sheet. You type occupancy, blocked nights, average stay, fee charged, cost paid and host fee %. The sheet calculates available and booked nights, turnovers, fees collected, platform cut on those fees, costs paid, annual net cleaning income, net margin per stay, the break-even cleaning fee, fee share of gross revenue, and the extra cleanings (and net dollars) if average stays drop by one night. It works the same in Excel, Google Sheets and LibreOffice. We tested every formula below by building a workbook with these exact formulas, recalculating it in LibreOffice, and comparing every cell to the worked example and to an independent Python model (plus dozens of randomized cases), and by cross-checking stays / fees / costs against the Short-Term Rental Analyzer SAMPLE Base column.

1. The Cleaning tab (inputs)

One sheet is enough. Type the yellow cells; the rest calculate.

A B
1 Occupancy % of available nights 62%
2 Blocked nights / year 14
3 Average stay (nights) 3.2
4 Cleaning fee charged per stay 95
5 Cleaning cost per turnover 85
6 Platform host fee % 15.5%
7 Average nightly rate (ADR) 185

These are the same Base-column SAMPLE inputs as the short-term rental deal analyzer tutorial ($185 ADR, 62% occupancy, 14 blocked nights, 3.2-night average stay, $95 fee, $85 cost, 15.5% host fee). ADR is only used here to show what share of gross the cleaning fees are; the net-margin math does not need it.

A few traps:

  • Occupancy is booked ÷ available nights, not ÷ 365. Blocked nights are owner stays and maintenance that cannot be booked.
  • Average stay sets how many turnovers you run. Shorter stays → more fees collected and more cleanings paid.
  • Platform host fee on cleaning: on Airbnb's single-fee structure, the service fee comes out of the host payout and applies to the nightly price plus fees you charge, such as the cleaning fee (Airbnb Help Center: service fees). That is why a $95 fee and an $85 cleaner can still lose money.
  • Lodging / occupancy taxes you collect and remit are not your revenue; leave them out of ADR and out of this sheet.

Label them in column A: Available nights, Booked nights, Stays (turnovers), Cleaning fees collected, Platform cut on cleaning fees, Cleaning costs paid, Net cleaning income (annual), Net margin per stay, Break-even cleaning fee, Cleaning fee share of gross, Stays if avg stay 1 night shorter, Extra cleanings vs current, Extra annual cleaning cost, Extra annual fees collected, Extra annual net cleaning.

What each one does:

  • B8–B10: available nights = 365 − blocked; booked = available × occupancy; stays = booked ÷ average stay (0 if stay is blank or 0).
  • B11–B13: fees guests pay × stays; the platform's cut of those fees (fees × host %); what you pay the cleaner × stays.
  • B14, annual net cleaning income: fees − platform cut − cleaner cost. This is the cleaning line's contribution before nightly revenue, fixed costs and the mortgage.
  • B15, net margin per stay: fee × (1 − host fee) − cost. Same sign as B14. Format as currency.
  • B16, break-even cleaning fee: the fee at which B15 is exactly 0: cost / (1 − host fee). Above this, each turnover adds cash after the platform cut; below it, each turnover costs you. Blank if the host fee is 100% or more.
  • B17, fee share of gross: cleaning fees ÷ (nightly revenue + cleaning fees). Format as a percentage. Useful when a listing looks "cheap" on ADR but the fee is doing the work — or when the fee is so high guests bounce.
  • B18–B22, one-night-shorter stress: if average stay drops by 1 night (holding booked nights fixed), how many extra turnovers, extra cleaner cost, extra fees collected, and extra net cleaning dollars. Blank when average stay is already 1 night or less (you cannot shorten further in this model).

Optional: stay-length what-if column

Put alternate average stays in D3:D8 (for example 2, 2.5, 3, 3.2, 4, 5) and this in E3, then fill down:

E3:  =IF(D3<=0,"",($B$9/D3)*($B$4*(1-$B$6)-$B$5))

That is annual net cleaning income at each stay length with everything else held at your inputs. It makes the "shorter stays look busier but can lose more on cleaning" point visible without retyping.

3. Why the host fee on the cleaning fee matters

Airbnb's help article on service fees states that under the single-fee structure the host fee is a percentage of the payout basis, including fees you charge such as cleaning. So the cash you keep from a $95 fee at 15.5% is $95 × (1 − 0.155) = $80.275, not $95. If the cleaner costs $85, each stay is −$4.725 on the cleaning line before you count anything else.

That does not mean "never charge a cleaning fee." It means price the fee as cleaner cost ÷ (1 − host fee) (or higher if you want the fee to contribute), and treat shorter stays as more turnovers of that margin — positive or negative.

What the example shows:

  1. Stays, fees and costs match the product SAMPLE Base column (68.00625 turnovers, $6,460.59 fees collected, $5,780.53 cleaning costs).
  2. Net margin per stay is −$4.73 even though $95 > $85, because 15.5% of $95 ($14.725) goes to the platform first.
  3. Break-even fee is $100.59. Raise the posted fee to at least that (or cut cleaner cost) before the cleaning line stops losing money.
  4. Cleaning fees are ~13.83% of gross in this sample ($6,460.59 of $46,720.29). Guests feel that; so does the host-fee base.
  5. If average stay drops from 3.2 to 2.2 nights, you run ~30.9 more cleanings. Extra fees collected look large (~$2,937), but extra net cleaning is still negative (~−$146) at these fee/cost numbers — shorter stays amplify a losing cleaning margin.

A second case where cleaning helps: keep everything the same but set the fee to $125 (put 125 in B4). Net margin per stay = $125 × 0.845 − $85 = $20.625. Annual net cleaning ≈ $1,402.63. Break-even fee is unchanged at $100.59.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing sticker fee to cleaner cost and stopping. Always subtract the host fee on the fee: fee × (1 − host%) − cost.
  • Using 365 as the occupancy base. Blocked owner/maintenance nights are not bookable; the deal analyzer and this sheet both use available nights.
  • Ignoring average stay. Two listings with the same booked nights and different stay lengths do not have the same cleaning P&L.
  • Raising the cleaning fee without watching the guest total. A high fee can clear the break-even line and still hurt occupancy if the all-in price looks worse than comps.
  • Forgetting that management software often forces the single-fee Airbnb structure. Check the current fee page for the account you actually use (Airbnb Help Center: service fees).
