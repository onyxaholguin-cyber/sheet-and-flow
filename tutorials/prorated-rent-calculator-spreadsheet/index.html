Prorated Rent Calculator Spreadsheet: Move-In and Move-Out Rent by the Day (Excel & Google Sheets)

Updated 2026-11-09 · Sheet & Flow

Tracking rent by hand? The Landlord Tenant Toolkit ($39) has the rent ledger the amount from this tutorial goes into.

When a tenant moves in on the 18th, nobody should pay a full month's rent for 14 days. Prorating means charging rent only for the days the tenant has the unit. The idea is simple, but the answer changes with the method you use, and the difference between methods is real money: on $1,650 rent, a December 18 move-in comes to $745.16, $759.45 or $770.00 depending on which of three common methods the lease uses.

This tutorial builds a small calculator that shows all three side by side, for the move-in month and the move-out month. It uses only DAY, EOMONTH, ROUND, MIN, IF, AND and OR, so it works the same in Excel, Google Sheets and LibreOffice. To test it, we built a workbook with these exact formulas, recalculated it in LibreOffice, and compared every result to the worked example below and to an independent Python model that counts the days one by one, for every possible move-in day from January 1, 2026 to December 31, 2028 (including the February 29, 2028 leap day) and for move-outs in the same month.

1. Settings

B1 (Monthly rent):   1650
B2 (Move-in date):   2026-12-18
B3 (Move-out date):  2027-02-09

Move-in date is the first day the tenant has the unit. Move-out date is the last day they have it (the last day they owe rent for). Leave B3 blank if nobody is moving out yet. Both days count as occupied, so a tenant who moves in on the 18th of a 31-day month pays for 14 days (the 18th through the 31st).

2. The move-in month

B6 (Days in move-in month):   =DAY(EOMONTH(B2,0))
B7 (Days occupied):           =IF(AND(B3<>"",EOMONTH(B3,0)=EOMONTH(B2,0)),B3-B2+1,EOMONTH(B2,0)-B2+1)
B8 (Actual days method):      =ROUND(B1*B7/B6,2)
B9 (365-day year method):     =ROUND(B1*12/365*B7,2)
B10 (Rent / 30 method):       =MIN(B1,ROUND(B1/30*B7,2))

EOMONTH(B2,0) is the last day of the move-in month in Excel (Microsoft's EOMONTH help) and in Google Sheets (Google's EOMONTH help), and DAY() of that date is the number of days in the month, so February, leap years and 30- versus 31-day months need no special case.

Days occupied is the last day of the month minus the move-in date, plus one because the move-in day counts. If the tenant also moves out in the same month, it's the move-out date minus the move-in date, plus one.

The three methods are:

  • Actual days: monthly rent × days occupied ÷ days in that month. The daily rate changes from month to month, and a full month always costs exactly one month's rent.
  • 365-day year: a yearly daily rate (monthly rent × 12 ÷ 365) × days occupied. The daily rate is the same all year.
  • Rent / 30: monthly rent ÷ 30 × days occupied, capped at one month's rent. This is the simplest by hand, but it charges more per day than the other two in 31-day months, which is why the cap is there.

All three round to the cent with ROUND(...,2). Use them only for a partial month. For a full month, charge the monthly rent: a February 1 move-in comes to $1,540.00 under rent ÷ 30 (28 days × $55) and a 31-day month to $1,681.64 under the 365-day year, neither of which is the rent.

3. The move-out month

B12 (Days in move-out month):  =IF(OR(B3="",EOMONTH(B3,0)=EOMONTH(B2,0)),"",DAY(EOMONTH(B3,0)))
B13 (Days occupied):           =IF(B12="","",DAY(B3))
B14 (Actual days method):      =IF(B12="","",ROUND(B1*B13/B12,2))
B15 (365-day year method):     =IF(B12="","",ROUND(B1*12/365*B13,2))
B16 (Rent / 30 method):        =IF(B12="","",MIN(B1,ROUND(B1/30*B13,2)))

In the move-out month the tenant has the unit from the 1st through the move-out date, so Days occupied is just the day of the month, DAY(B3). The section stays blank when B3 is empty, and also when the move-out is in the move-in month, because the move-in section already counted those days.

Worked example

Rent is $1,650. The tenant moves in on Friday, December 18, 2026 and moves out on Tuesday, February 9, 2027.

Month Days in month Days occupied Actual days 365-day year Rent / 30
December 2026 (move-in) 31 14 745.16 759.45 770.00
February 2027 (move-out) 28 9 530.36 488.22 495.00

What the numbers show:

  1. The method matters most in long and short months. In December (31 days) the actual-days method is the cheapest for the tenant at $745.16, and rent ÷ 30 is the most expensive at $770.00, a $24.84 difference for the same 14 days.
  2. February flips it. With only 28 days, the actual-days daily rate is the highest of the year, so 9 days come to $530.36 under that method but $488.22 under the 365-day year.
  3. In a 30-day month, actual days and rent ÷ 30 agree. A November 18 move-in is 13 days either way: $1,650 × 13 ÷ 30 = $715.00 (the 365-day year method gives $705.21).

January 2027 is a full month in the example, so it's simply $1,650 under any method.

A move-in and move-out in the same month

If the tenant moves in on March 5, 2027 and out on March 20, 2027, B7 counts 16 days (the 5th through the 20th) and the move-out section stays blank. With $1,650 rent that's $851.61 by actual days (31-day month), $867.95 by the 365-day year and $880.00 by rent ÷ 30.

Which method should you use?

Use the one your lease says. If the lease doesn't say, the actual-days method is the easiest to explain because a full month always equals one month's rent and the daily rate is just that month's rent split evenly. Whatever you pick, write it into the lease and use the same method for every tenant, so the move-in and move-out math can't turn into a dispute.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the move-in day. Subtracting the dates (31 − 18 = 13) undercounts by one day. The +1 in B7 counts both the move-in day and the last day of the month.
  • Using 30 days for every month without a cap. Rent ÷ 30 × 31 days is more than a month's rent. The MIN(B1, ...) in B10 and B16 keeps a full month at one month's rent.
  • Counting the move-out day twice or not at all. Decide whether the move-out date is the last day the tenant owes rent for (as in this sheet) or the day the keys come back, and put that in the lease.
  • Dates typed as text. If B7 shows #VALUE!, a date was pasted as text. Re-enter it as a real date (in Google Sheets, Format → Number → Date).
