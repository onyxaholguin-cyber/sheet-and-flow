Invoice Follow-Up Schedule Spreadsheet: Which Overdue Invoices to Chase Today, and When Next (Excel & Google Sheets)

Updated 2026-10-30 · Sheet & Flow

Short on time? The Invoice Chaser for n8n ($12) is the finished, tested version of what this tutorial builds.

Chasing late invoices fails in two predictable ways: you forget an invoice for three weeks, or you send two reminders in two days and annoy a client who was about to pay. The fix is a schedule, not willpower. Each unpaid invoice gets at most three reminders (friendly, firm, final), each one only after the invoice is far enough past due, and never two reminders closer together than a few days.

This tutorial builds that schedule as one sheet. You keep the invoice list you already have and add two columns you update when you send a reminder (how many you've sent, and when). The sheet then works out, for every invoice, the next reminder date and what to do today: send reminder 1, 2 or 3, wait, skip (paid, disputed, on hold) or call the client. It works the same in Excel, Google Sheets and LibreOffice, using only WORKDAY, MAX, INDEX, MATCH, IF, COUNTIF and SUMIF.

To test it, we built a workbook with these exact formulas, recalculated it in LibreOffice and compared every cell to the worked example below and to an independent Python model, on the example plus 40 randomized invoice lists (partial payments, every stop status, weekend as-of dates, reminders already sent, cooldowns that end on a Saturday). We also ran the same invoices through the decision logic of our Invoice Chaser automation, day by day, and checked that the first day it would send each reminder is the date this sheet predicts.

The rules the sheet follows

Rule Default Why
Reminder 1 3 days past due Gives a few days for payments already in transit
Reminder 2 10 days past due Clear and specific: amount, due date, how to pay
Reminder 3 (final) 21 days past due Last automated note before a personal call
Cooldown 5 days since the last reminder A late first reminder never triggers the next one two days later
Weekends No sends Saturday or Sunday The next send moves to Monday
Stop statuses Paid, Void, Cancelled, Disputed, Hold, On hold, Do not chase, Written off Never chase an invoice you've agreed to pause
After 3 reminders Call the client More templates rarely help; a conversation does

Days are counted from the due date, not the invoice date. A reminder is due when both conditions hold: the invoice is at least the stage's number of days overdue, and at least the cooldown has passed since the last reminder.

Layout

Settings at the top of the tab:

Cell Meaning Example
B1 As-of date (=TODAY() or a typed date) 2026-10-30
B2 Cooldown days 5
D1, E1, F1 Days overdue for reminders 1, 2, 3 3, 10, 21

Invoices start on row 5 (headers on row 4): A Invoice #, B Client, C Due date, D Amount, E Amount paid, F Status, G Reminders sent, H Last reminder sent. You type A to H; leave G and H blank for a new invoice. The formulas go in I to M and fill down.

The formulas

Balance still owed (partial payments count):

I5 (Balance):  =D5-E5

Days overdue (negative means not due yet):

J5 (Days overdue):  =$B$1-C5

Stop status. MATCH is not case-sensitive, so disputed and Disputed both stop the chase:

K5 (Stop?):  =ISNUMBER(MATCH(TRIM(F5),{"Paid","Void","Cancelled","Canceled","Disputed","Hold","On hold","Do not chase","Written off"},0))

Next reminder date. The earliest day both rules allow is the later of (due date + the next stage's days) and (last reminder + cooldown), and never earlier than the as-of date. WORKDAY(date-1,1) keeps a weekday as it is and moves a Saturday or Sunday to the following Monday. Blank when there's nothing left to send:

L5 (Next reminder):  =IF(OR(K5,I5<=0.005,G5>=3),"",WORKDAY(MAX(C5+INDEX($D$1:$F$1,G5+1),IF(H5="",0,H5+$B$2),$B$1)-1,1))

What to do today:

M5 (Action):  =IF(K5,"Skip ("&TRIM(F5)&")",IF(I5<=0.005,"Paid",IF(G5>=3,"Call the client",IF(L5=$B$1,"Send reminder "&(G5+1),"Wait"))))

Format C, H and L as dates. Two summary cells next to the settings tell you how big today's job is:

J1 (Reminders to send today):  =COUNTIF(M5:M200,"Send*")
J2 (Balance to chase today):  =SUMIF(M5:M200,"Send*",I5:I200)

After you send a reminder, add 1 to G and put today's date in H. The row then shows Wait with the date of the next stage, or Call the client after the third.

Worked example

As-of date Friday, October 30, 2026, default stages 3/10/21 and a 5-day cooldown:

Invoice # Due date Amount Paid Status Sent Last sent Balance Days overdue Next reminder Action
INV-101 2026-10-20 1200.00 0.00 1 2026-10-23 1200.00 10 2026-10-30 Send reminder 2
INV-102 2026-10-26 800.00 0.00 0 800.00 4 2026-10-30 Send reminder 1
INV-103 2026-10-01 2500.00 1000.00 2 2026-10-27 1500.00 29 2026-11-02 Wait
INV-104 2026-10-29 450.00 0.00 0 450.00 1 2026-11-02 Wait
INV-105 2026-09-15 600.00 0.00 3 2026-10-06 600.00 45 Call the client
INV-106 2026-10-05 900.00 0.00 Disputed 1 2026-10-08 900.00 25 Skip (Disputed)
INV-107 2026-10-10 950.00 950.00 1 2026-10-13 0.00 20 Paid
INV-108 2026-11-15 300.00 0.00 0 300.00 -16 2026-11-18 Wait

Today's job: 2 reminders for $2,000.00 (reminder 2 to INV-101 and reminder 1 to INV-102).

The interesting rows are the ones that wait:

  • INV-103 is 29 days overdue, well past the 21-day final stage, but its second reminder went out on October 27. The cooldown ends Sunday, November 1, so the final reminder moves to Monday, November 2, 2026. Without the cooldown rule it would have gone out three days after the last one.
  • INV-104 reaches 3 days overdue on Sunday, November 1, so reminder 1 also waits for Monday.
  • INV-107 has a blank status but a zero balance, so it shows Paid. Checking the balance, not just the status, stops you from chasing a client who already paid in full.
  • INV-106 is disputed: the sheet skips it however late it is. Sort out the dispute by talking to the client, then clear the status.

Changing the rules

  • Different stages. Change D1:F1, for example 7/14/30 for slower-paying corporate clients. Keep them in increasing order.
  • Longer cooldown. Set B2 to 7 for a weekly rhythm.
  • Public holidays. Add a holiday range as WORKDAY's third argument (WORKDAY(...-1,1,$P$5:$P$20)) and the next reminder skips those days too. See the WORKDAY help in Excel or Google Sheets.
  • A fourth stage. Add a value in G1, change $D$1:$F$1 to $D$1:$G$1 and the two 3s in L5 and M5 to 4.

Common mistakes

  • Counting from the invoice date. Net-30 terms mean day 0 is the due date. Counting from the invoice date sends a "late" reminder before the invoice is due.
  • Chasing the original amount. After a partial payment, quote the balance (I), not the invoice total.
  • Not updating Sent and Last sent. The schedule only moves forward when you record each reminder. Forget it and the sheet keeps showing Send reminder 1.
  • Sending on a weekend. A Saturday reminder sits unread until Monday under a pile of other mail. The sheet moves it to Monday for you.
  • Automating past three reminders. After the final one, M says Call the client. A fourth template email almost never works better than a short call.
